# Positioning — placing the client on the strategy

## The move

1. **Start from the business.** List the goals in priority order with their destinations (site, cart, booking page). The account exists to send the right people there.
2. **Borrow the lane.** The #1-goal bucket's model shows a lane that already grows in the target community. The client enters that lane, not a new one.
3. **Bring the edge.** The client's unique content is what the model cannot copy. It becomes either a differentiator inside the #1-goal bucket or an edge bucket.
4. **Assign jobs.** Every kept bucket has one job tied to a goal and its destination:
   - **reach** — puts the name in front of strangers (usually quote-reacts on what the community already looks at)
   - **conversion** — turns a profile visit into a follow/inquiry (usually own work, shown, ideally as short video)
   - **differentiation** — makes the account the only one of its kind (the unique bucket)
5. **Route.** Conversion posts point to a destination via the first self-reply (never the post). Each bucket points to its own goal's link; the bio/pinned post points to the #1 goal.
6. **Sequence.** Reach first, then conversion while the traffic is live; differentiation at low volume, compounding.

## Category decision — solve it, never assume it

The category is what a stranger files the account under ("agency", "designer", "DJ", "tool"). The client's current label (bio, Brain, company name) is **one candidate, not the answer**. Decide it before writing any positioning line.

1. **Anchor on the final landing page.** From intake: where every path ends and what the visitor does there. The category must make that page the obvious next click. A label that makes the landing page feel like a bait-and-switch loses, however well it reaches.
2. **List 3–4 candidates** that the client could truthfully own. Always include: the current label, the model account's category for the #1-goal bucket, a person-led label (e.g. "<craft> who <does X>"), and a label built on the edge. Do not invent capabilities to fit a label.
3. **Score each candidate** (one line of evidence per cell; mark unmeasured):

   | Test | Evidence source |
   |---|---|
   | **Landing fit** — does a visitor arriving under this label expect what the landing page sells? | landing page copy (fetch it, free) vs the label |
   | **Model proof** — did the #1-goal model grow under this kind of label? | model bio + top posts' framing |
   | **Own proof** — which framing did the client's best posts carry? | own corpus top-20 by likes/views, tagged by framing |
   | **Buyer match** — is it how the #1-goal buyer searches/hires? | intake goal + offer wording; Market Signals keywords if present |
   | **Edge room** — can every kept bucket live under it without confusing the label? | blend verdicts |
   | **Crowding** — how many look-alike accounts already own it? | judgement; label "unmeasured" |

4. **Pick one.** Winner → `Category To Own`. The other candidates (and anything the data shows hurts) → `Category To Avoid`, each with a short reason. Record the scored table in `Strategy Document › Decisions` (dated), so the owner can overrule it with the reasoning in view.
5. **If the winner conflicts with the landing page** (e.g. the data says "designer-builder" but the page sells "agency services"), do not bend the category — raise it as an owner question: change the landing page copy, or accept a weaker label. The skill never edits the site.

## Outputs the positioning step must produce

| Output | Brain field |
|---|---|
| One positioning sentence: who it's for, what the client does, the edge | Identity › Positioning |
| What the account should be known for / never be known for | Identity › Category To Own / Category To Avoid |
| Edge statements (one per bullet, concrete) | Identity › Differentiators |
| One line for X bios/headers | Identity › X-Oriented One-Liner |
| Strategic one-liner (internal) | Identity › Strategic One-Liner |
| Thesis — what the account is for + the one rule everything follows | Strategy Document › Thesis |
| Who it reaches and what they come for | Strategy Document › Audience + Identity › Primary Audience |

Tests for the positioning sentence: a stranger can repeat it; it names the #1-goal audience; it contains the edge; it excludes something.

## Authority angle

The strongest edge is first-hand: the client **made** it, **was there**, or **owns** it. Models who curate cannot claim that. Prefer naming the specific artifact, place, or era over a job title — precision is the credibility signal for expert audiences, and a wrong detail costs more than a vague one.

## Worked example (illustration only — do not copy values)

- **Goals:** #1 design/development clients → contact page; secondary: record sales → shop, bookings → booking link.
- **Buckets:** (A) design & build work, tools, Studio output · (B) underground electronic music — mixes, records, flyers, scene archive.
- **Models:** A → @seb__design (solo design freelancer, 0→1.5k in ~2 months via quote-reacts + own-work video) · B → record/scene accounts.
- **Diagnosis:** own showcase engagement ≥ model's, median views ~1/30th → distribution problem, not content.
- **Category (illustrative — re-run the decision):** candidates agency / design studio / designer-builder / music-tech builder, scored against the final landing page.
- **Positioning:** a designer-builder who ships the tools, with a decade inside the music scene — "he curates, you generate"; "I was there" beats commentary.
- **Blend:** A core (#1 goal, conversion + reach). B edge — unique, credible, shares a visual-taste community — at a capped share; own-account only if its lift stays low and overlap measures near zero.
- **Never:** market/speculation talk (worst-measured lane), promo posts as a post type.
