## 0. Operating frame (the constraint every angle must respect)

Per the canonical positioning: HITLOOP is **not** a SaaS product. It is Bryan's portfolio + onboarding front door for a **human-accountable creative service**. So every angle below is scored against one rule:

> **Does it sell Bryan's judgment, curation, and accountability — or does it turn HITLOOP into a tool people buy?**
> The first is on-strategy. The second is off-strategy and only kept here as a flagged "future fork."

**Market context that shapes all angles (researched 2026):** AI has *collapsed* the price of commodity creative work — AI-first tools at $59–$109/mo now do what a $1,500–$3,000/mo freelancer did, and a 10-video campaign that cost $100k+ through agencies is now under $100. ([Apaya](https://apaya.com/blog/ai-social-media-management-costs), [AutoFaceless](https://autofaceless.ai/blog/ai-video-generation-statistics-2026)). **Implication:** Bryan must NOT compete on per-asset price. The defensible money is in *judgment, stewardship, and outcomes* — exactly the positioning. Commodity deliverables (scheduling, single videos) should be **bundled into a stewarded retainer, never sold as cheap one-offs.**

## 2. The angles, in detail

### Angle 1 — The diagnostic wedge → creative retainer ★★★★★ (lead motion)
**Offer:** a free, personalized baseline of a prospect's business (brand read + content-gap + sample of what consistent output looks like for *their* brand) that converts into a content retainer.
**Repo:** the shipping Deliverables bucket already produces this — Creative Brief (`onboarding-brief`), Visual Audit (`style-guide`), Social Preview, Multi-Device Mock. The diagnostic is assembled, not hand-built.
**Market:** free audits are among the highest-converting lead magnets in service businesses at **18–32%**, and function as "lead magnet, sales tool, and qualification filter in one." Prospects who complete an audit are pre-qualified and already understand they need help. ([Auditsky](https://auditsky.ai/newsletter/website-audits-that-win-clients-how-agencies-turn-traffic-into-leads/), [Venture Harbour](https://ventureharbour.com/10-lead-magnets/)).
**Why it wins:** it weaponizes the one thing the system uniquely does — diagnosing a business *before contact*. Already the core of the rollout strategy. **This is the spine; most other angles attach to it.**

### Angle 2 — Fractional creative director / content steward ★★★★★ (the named role)
**Offer:** position Bryan as a fractional creative director who *curates and stewards an agentic content system* on the client's behalf, accountable for the output. This is the exact job title the market already pays for — and it matches the positioning word-for-word.
**Repo:** the whole platform is the "system he stewards." Nothing new to build; this is how to *name and price* the offer.
**Market:** fractional CDs command **$99k–$250k** annualized, typically run **2–4 clients at 10–20 hrs/week**, with engagements of **6–12 months**. Crucially, the market already frames the role as *brand stewardship* (~30% of their time) and explicitly values someone who "wields AI like a scalpel, not a sledgehammer… bringing human context and brand stewardship AI can't replicate." ([Mediabistro](https://www.mediabistro.com/be-inspired/advice-from-the-pros/why-marketing-teams-are-hiring-creative-directors-part-time-and-how-it-actually-works/), [Content Rewired](https://contentrewired.com/fractional-content-marketing-for-the-ai-era/), [ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Fractional-Creative-Director)).
**Why it wins:** it gives the pitch a category buyers already understand and budget for, and it is inherently human-accountable. **Recommended as the headline offer; the diagnostic wedge is how you sell it.**

### Angle 3 — GEO / AI-SEO readiness audit + remediation ★★★★★ (highest-value attach)
**Offer:** audit a client's visibility in AI search (ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, Perplexity) and remediate. Sold as a premium project or recurring engagement.
**Repo:** you already have the tooling and a worked example — `ai-seo-audit/`, `docs/seo/GEO-ANALYSIS.md` (a full GEO scorecard methodology), plus agent-readiness checks in the scout analyzers. You've done this on your own site; you can do it for clients.
**Market:** this is the single highest-willingness-to-pay angle on the list. **31.3% of the US population will use generative AI search in 2026.** GEO retainers run **$2,000–$8,000/mo mid-market** (chaotically up to $50k+), priced **20–40% above traditional SEO** because it's "an engineering challenge, not a copywriting exercise." LLM-cited visitors convert at **5.7× the lead-to-close velocity** of Google organic. ([WebFX](https://www.webfx.com/blog/ai/generative-engine-optimization-cost/), [Fuel Online](https://fuelonline.com/ai-seo-geo/ai-seo-pricing-aeo-geo-cost-2026/), [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/faq-on-geo-aeo--where-ai-search-seo-overlap-2026)).
**Why it wins:** defensible (technical), premium-priced, you already own the methodology, and it's a perfect *second* conversation after the free diagnostic. **Recommended as the high-margin attach to the retainer.**

### Angle 4 — AI-managed social content engine (done-for-you retainer) ★★★★
**Offer:** the recurring deliverable underneath the retainer — the system produces baseline social posts, images, video, and runs regular promos, with Bryan accountable for quality and brand fit.
**Repo:** Strategy Builder (30-day post calendar) + Social Posting automation (cron auto-poster + Guardian content-safety QA) + Post Me + Video Promo. **Caveat: Strategy Builder is WIP-partial** — its data aggregation is shallow and the source-toggle UI is incomplete; Social Posting core is built but engagement tracking isn't. This is the engine to finish.
**Market:** SMM retainers run **$1,000–$6,000/mo**; a productized monthly content calendar with captions + images is **$1,000–$2,500**. ([Feedbird](https://feedbird.com/blog/social-media-management-cost), [Eclincher](https://www.eclincher.com/articles/social-media-management-pricing-rates-and-costs-for-2026)).
**Why it's ★★★★ not ★★★★★:** the value here is commoditizing fast — never sell it standalone on price. Bundle it inside the stewarded retainer (Angle 2) where the human accountability is the differentiator.

### Angle 5 — Short-form video promo packs ★★★ (high pull, commoditizing)
**Offer:** branded 30-second promo videos / monthly video packs.
**Repo:** Video Promo (`mockup-studio`, Cloud Run GPU) + Video Remix (EditVideos bridge) — both shipping (gated by EditVideos auth).
**Market:** enormous demand — AI video market **~$18.6B by end of 2026**, short-form is **67%** of AI-generated video, **SMBs are 46% of AI-video signups**, and median production cost has fallen from **$4,200 → $2,500/finished minute** (campaigns 70–90% cheaper). ([AutoFaceless](https://autofaceless.ai/blog/ai-video-generation-statistics-2026), [Vivideo](https://vivideo.ai/blog/ai-video-statistics-2026)).
**Why it's ★★★:** demand is huge but prices are collapsing and competitors are everywhere. Use video as a **wow-factor inclusion** in the retainer and the diagnostic (a sample promo for *their* brand), not a price-shopped SKU.

### Angle 6 — Brand snapshot / visual audit as a paid mini-deliverable ★★★
**Offer:** a low-priced, fast brand-system extraction + visual consistency audit. Good as a paid tripwire or qualifier.
**Repo:** Visual Audit (`style-guide`) — extracts palette, type, tokens; verifies against the live homepage. Shipping.
**Why:** cheap to deliver, useful top-of-funnel, and a natural upsell ramp into the retainer. Low ceiling on its own.

### Angle 7 — Local reputation service (GBP) ★★★★ (build to unlock)
**Offer:** monitor Google Business Profile reviews, draft on-brand replies, run a local-SEO checklist, flag reputation risk.
**Repo:** GBP Reputation card — **planned, 6 phases, not built** (`docs/plans/GBP-REPUTATION-CARD-PLAN.md`).
**Market:** strong and local-business-shaped (your ICP). **70%+ of local searches result in GBP interactions**; businesses that respond to reviews get **68% more clicks**; **88% of multi-location operators see GBP results but only 20% can measure them** — a clear service gap. AI Overviews now name local businesses with complete profiles. ([Gravitas Vision](https://www.gravitasvision.com/post/the-2026-google-business-profile-playbook-beyond-basic-listings), [Dyad](https://dyadmarketing.com/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-checklist-for-2026)).
**Why it's ★★★★:** perfect fit for the local-business ICP (the poke-shop archetype), and pairs naturally with Angle 3 (GEO) since both are about getting cited in AI/local search. Needs build — prioritize if the early clients are local.

### Angle 8 — Leadgen as Bryan's own prospecting engine ★★★★★ (internal weapon, not a product)
**Offer (to himself, not to clients):** point the leadgen pipeline at local businesses with weak web presences → score → audit → auto-generate a preview redesign + personalized diagnostic. This is how to mass-produce the Angle 1 wedge for the direct-outreach motion in the rollout.
**Repo:** Leadgen pipeline — **planned, architecture complete** (`docs/features/leadgen/LEAD_GEN_PIPELINE_PLAN.md`): Discover → Score → Audit → Generate.
**Why it's ★★★★★:** it's not a thing to sell — it's the machine that fills Bryan's pipeline with pre-diagnosed prospects, which is the single biggest lever on the whole rollout. Finishing this scales outreach from hand-picked 10–20 to a repeatable engine. **Highest-leverage WIP to finish for growth.**

### Angle 9 — Knowledge Base / Client Brain = the consistency moat ★★★★ (enabler, not sold)
**Offer:** none directly. This is the capability that lets the system produce on-brand, personalized content *without Bryan in the loop* — the literal precondition for his end-state pitch ("I'm confident enough to let my own social run on the system I curated").
**Repo:** Knowledge Base (ingest + retrieval, embeddings pending) + Client Brain (context pack, WIP-partial). Note: the first Company Brain doc (Capability Profile) already lives here.
**Why it matters:** stewardship doesn't scale past ~2–4 clients without this. It's the difference between Bryan doing the work and Bryan *curating a system that does the work*. **Finish it to make Angle 2 scalable.**

### Angle 10 — Arweave archival / provenance add-on ★★ (niche differentiator)
**Offer:** permanent, provenance-stamped content archive + Arweave-hosted microsite for clients who value it (creators, artists, Web3 brands).
**Repo:** Archive & Publishing (read-only bridge + cost estimation shipping; deploy phases partial).
**Why it's ★★:** genuinely unique and on-brand for Bryan's Web3 background, but a narrow audience. Keep as a premium niche upsell, not a core motion.

### Angle 11 — White-label / license the system ★ (FUTURE FORK — off current strategy)
**Offer:** license HITLOOP to other creatives/agencies as white-label tooling.
**Market:** large and proven — white-label AI platforms like Vendasta support **60,000+ resellers**; onboarding alone bills **$1,500–$5,000/client** plus monthly optimization retainers. ([ALM Corp](https://almcorp.com/blog/white-label-ai-marketing-services-agencies-2026-guide/), [Insighto](https://insighto.ai/blog/best-ai-white-label-services/)).
**Why it's ★ (flagged, not recommended now):** this directly contradicts the current positioning ("not a SaaS, it's my portfolio"). It's a real future option *if* Bryan ever wants to productize — but pursuing it now would dilute the human-accountable service brand. **Documented for completeness; revisit only as a deliberate pivot.**

## 3. Recommended angle stack (what the research says to actually run)

A coherent offer ladder, not a menu:

1. **Headline role:** Fractional creative director / content steward (Angle 2) — the category buyers understand and budget for.
2. **Front door:** the personalized diagnostic wedge (Angle 1) — free, pre-qualifies, converts at 18–32%.
3. **High-margin attach:** GEO / AI-SEO readiness (Angle 3) — premium, defensible, you already own the method.
4. **Recurring engine:** AI-managed social + video, bundled into the retainer (Angles 4 + 5) — never sold on price.
5. **Local add-on where the ICP is local:** GBP reputation (Angle 7).
6. **Internal growth weapon to finish first:** Leadgen self-prospecting (Angle 8).
7. **Moat to finish for scale:** Knowledge Base / Client Brain (Angle 9).

**Build priority for WIP features, ranked by revenue leverage:** (1) Leadgen self-prospecting → fills the pipeline; (2) Knowledge Base / Client Brain → makes stewardship scale; (3) Strategy Builder data/UI gaps → makes the recurring engine deliverable; (4) GBP Reputation → unlocks the local add-on.

**Anti-pattern to avoid:** selling any single deliverable (a video, a calendar, an audit) as a cheap standalone. The market has driven those to near-zero. Every deliverable is a *proof point inside* the stewarded relationship, not the product.

## 4. Company Brain document registry + repeatable onboarding process

This thread is also the **template for client onboarding** — the sequence of docs we produce here is the spine we'll later generate *per client* (and eventually dynamically). Tracking it meticulously is the point.

**Documents produced so far (HITLOOP's own brain):**

| # | Document | Role in the brain | Status |
|---|----------|-------------------|--------|
| 1 | Capability Profile (Claude vs HITLOOP) | What the human/AI does vs what the system does — division of labor | First doc in Company Brain card |
| 2 | Positioning Statement | Canonical reposition — source of truth all else obeys | Preliminary v0.1 |
| 3 | Rollout Strategy | Go-to-market: how to land creative clients | Draft |
| 4 | Angles Exploration (this doc) | All monetization/positioning angles, repo + market grounded | Preliminary |

**The repeatable spine (apply this same document set to each future client):**

1. **Capability map** — what their business + our system can/can't do (the division of labor).
2. **Positioning statement** — who they are, what they're really selling, what they're not.
3. **Diagnostic baseline** — the auto-generated gap read (the wedge).
4. **Angle/offer ladder** — the specific services/deliverables that fit *their* gap and market.
5. **Rollout plan** — how their content engine gets stood up and stewarded.

**Why track it this way:** once the spine is fixed and each doc has a known shape, the inputs (client URL, vertical, brand voice) can be fed to the system to *generate the first three docs automatically* — which is exactly the "apply the process to other clients, dynamically" goal. The Knowledge Base / Client Brain (Angle 9) is the mechanism that will eventually template this. For now, we produce them by hand in threads like this one and store each in the Company Brain card.
